Upfront Pricing

Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Trenton Water Heater provides honest, upfront pricing on every job, covering everything from a burned-out heating element to a complete tank replacement. Every visit begins with a careful inspection of your water heater, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and valve and tank condition, followed by a clear recommendation and a written price before any work starts.

A lot of water heater failures give quiet warnings long before the tank quits. Watch for hot water with a rusty tint, a rumbling tank, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, or showers that go cold sooner than they used to. Behind those symptoms are usually heavy sediment, a depleted anode rod, or a worn dip tube. A quick inspection at the first sign usually costs far less than cleaning up after a failed tank.

Not every call is an emergency. Plenty of homeowners ask us to size up an aging heater before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical tank lifespan of 8 to 12 years (tankless units often run 20 years with annual descaling), replacing on your own schedule beats a rushed swap after a leak. We'll give you an honest read on how much life your equipment has left.

Annual Flushing

Maintenance Plans for Trenton Homes

Preventing a breakdown beats paying for one. That's the idea behind our annual maintenance. Once a year, we drain and flush the tank, measure the anode rod, test the relief valve, inspect the gas valve and venting or the elements and wiring, and run a descaling cycle on tankless units, all matched to the heater in your home.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with annual service for most tanks and twice-yearly flushing where the water is hard. This is especially important in hard-water areas, where sediment insulates the tank bottom, forces the burner to run longer, and wears out the tank. Each home is assessed individually, and our technicians tailor the plan to your equipment's age, your household's hot water needs, and your local water.

Think of maintenance as a safeguard for equipment you've already paid for. Each visit keeps the tank clear of sediment, the anode rod working, and the relief valve in working order, and that lowers monthly energy bills while reducing wear on the burner or elements. For most families, maintenance costs far less than replacing the heater ahead of schedule.

Right-Sized Hot Water

Hot Water Sized to Your Family's Demand

Getting the size right matters more than most homeowners realize. Before recommending a new heater, Trenton Water Heater looks at the details that determine your household's peak demand: the number of people and bathrooms, back-to-back showers, laundry and dishwasher timing, and how cold the water entering the house gets. That gives you a unit with a first-hour rating or GPM capacity to cover your morning rush without wasting energy on excess capacity.

Households change over time. A growing family or an added bathroom can overwhelm the tank that used to be plenty. A tankless unit heats water on demand and never runs out as long as the flow stays within its GPM rating, while hybrid heat pump tanks draw far less electricity than a standard electric tank. Whether you're comparing tank and tankless, or gas and electric, we'll lay out the real trade-offs in upfront cost, operating cost, venting and gas line needs, and installation work.

Common Problems

The Water Heater Repairs Local Homes Need Most

Most service calls we take come down to a handful of familiar problems. The water never gets hot. The pilot lights and then goes out a minute later. The hot water runs out fast, the tank rumbles and pops, or a tankless unit starts flashing an error code. Rusty water, a rotten-egg smell, and a dripping relief valve round out the list. Our technicians handle all of them, always targeting the component that actually failed.

Not every water heater problem is equally urgent. A failed element only cuts your hot water supply. A relief valve spitting water, on the other hand, is a safety device doing its job and telling you something is wrong, and water pooling under the tank can soak into subfloors and drywall before anyone notices. Smell gas? Leave the house and call your gas utility before anything else. Our technicians carry the parts to swap elements, thermostats, thermocouples, gas valves, and relief valves, install expansion tanks where pressure is the problem, and handle leak repair at the drain valve or fittings.
