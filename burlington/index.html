A little caution before we arrive can head off a much bigger mess. For a leak, close the cold supply valve at the top of the tank, then switch off the breaker for an electric model or set the gas control to "off" on a gas model. Move boxes and anything valuable away from the tank and lay towels around the base to keep the water contained. Gas smells are never a wait-and-see situation: leave and call the gas utility from outside.

How Burlington Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Most premature tank failures start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Drain and flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and test the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The rating plate on the tank usually includes the manufacture date, and its age says a lot about what comes next. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that point and is also showing rusty water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install once the tank fails.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A tank that's just begun to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet the same sediment, ignored through a few more years, can create hot spots on the tank floor and burn out the lower element on an electric model, forcing a replacement much sooner. Save our number on your phone now so help is one call away the moment something seems off. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.
