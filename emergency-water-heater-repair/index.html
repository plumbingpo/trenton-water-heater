Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Water at the tap that is suddenly scalding hot
  • Any gas odor around the heater, which means getting out and calling the gas company before calling us
  • The relief valve on the side of the tank dripping, hissing, or discharging
  • Loud popping, banging, or rumbling from the tank that has gotten worse quickly
  • Every tap running cold and the heater showing no signs of life
  • A puddle under the tank that keeps growing
  • Black soot or heat discoloration around the burner door or draft hood
  • An electric water heater that keeps tripping its circuit breaker

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

The risks in a water heater emergency are easy to underestimate. In reality, you may be dealing with standing water beside 240-volt wiring, a gas supply and open burner a few inches from a puddle, and a tank of very hot water under pressure. The relief valve, if it is firing, is the final line of defense between an overheated tank and a violent failure, and plugging it is one of the most dangerous things anyone can do to a heater. Relighting a pilot while gas lingers, or forcing a rusted connection, can make things much worse. Leave, shut off, and call is the right sequence for anyone without the tools and training.

A trained technician brings the right order of operations and the right parts. Our team confirms the heater is isolated, tests for gas leaks with a detector and soap solution instead of relying on a sniff test, checks household water pressure and thermal expansion when a relief valve has been discharging, and inspects the vent for proper draft before a gas unit is restarted. Just as important, we tell you honestly when a heater can be repaired and when it cannot. A leaking seam or rusted-through bottom cannot be fixed, while a failed valve, fitting, or element usually can, and the difference is often a few hundred dollars versus a new heater. You get that answer before any work begins.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Find and label the cold-water shutoff at the heater and the main house valve now, before you ever need them
  • Annual flushing keeps sediment from causing the rumbling and hot spots that lead to leaks
  • Store chemicals and flammables far from the burner, and keep the area around the heater uncluttered
  • A simple water sensor on the floor beside the tank can warn you hours earlier
  • Test the T&P valve annually; a valve that sticks or weeps afterward should be replaced
  • Set the temperature around 120F, which protects against burns and slows tank wear
  • Ask about a drain pan and drain line, especially for heaters in attics, closets, or above finished rooms
