Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Camden

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling every year if their water is hard.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually encodes the manufacture date, and that date says a lot about what comes next. Most tank models make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units about 20. If your heater is beyond that point and also shows discolored water, popping sounds, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than a rushed emergency install after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip ignored for months can keep stressing the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Typical Water Heater Issues in Camden

Whatever type of water heater your Camden home has, it tends to fail in a predictable way. Gas tanks usually give trouble through pilot assemblies and thermocouples, sooty burners, and venting that doesn't draft. Electric tanks lose heating elements and thermostats, particularly when sediment piles up around the lower element. Tankless units show error codes for scale buildup, ignition failures, and flow sensor trouble, and many of those trace back to skipped descaling. Heat pump water heaters depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they don't do well in small, closed-in spaces. Our technicians work on all four every week and carry the parts to fix most problems on the first visit.
